Ethical Principles

JTEM is committed to ethical, transparent, and accountable scholarly publishing. The journal aligns its practices with international standards, including guidance from COPE, while maintaining original policies tailored to medical and translational research.

Authorship and Contributorship

Authorship requires substantial intellectual contribution, participation in drafting or revising the work, and approval of the final manuscript. All contributors must be properly acknowledged, and authorship changes after submission require written consent from all authors.

Conflicts of Interest

Authors, reviewers, and editors must disclose financial or non-financial conflicts that could influence interpretation. Disclosures are published with the article when relevant.

Plagiarism and Duplicate Publication

Manuscripts are screened for plagiarism and redundant publication. Text recycling or duplicate submission is grounds for rejection or retraction.

Data Fabrication and Falsification

Fabrication, falsification, or selective reporting of data is unacceptable. Suspected cases are investigated and may result in rejection, retraction, or notification to institutions.

Citation Manipulation

The journal does not tolerate coercive citation practices or inappropriate manipulation of references for metric inflation. References must be relevant and support the scientific record.

Complaints and Appeals

Complaints about editorial decisions, reviewer conduct, or publication ethics may be submitted to the editorial office. Appeals are reviewed by independent editors and documented in the journal’s system.

Corrections, Retractions, and Expressions of Concern

JTEM issues corrections for honest errors, retractions for unreliable findings or misconduct, and expressions of concern when investigations are ongoing. Notices are linked to the original articles and remain publicly accessible.

Ethics Committee Approval

Studies involving human participants or animals must provide ethics committee approval details and compliance with applicable regulations. Manuscripts lacking documented approval will not proceed to review.

Informed Consent

Authors must confirm informed consent for clinical studies, including consent for publication of identifiable information. For vulnerable populations, additional safeguards are required.

Clinical Trial Registration

Prospective clinical trials must be registered in a publicly accessible registry prior to patient enrollment. Registration numbers must be included in the manuscript.

Patient Privacy and Confidentiality

Identifiable patient data must be anonymized unless explicit consent is provided. Images or case details that could reveal identity require documented permission.

Use of AI Tools in Research and Writing

Use of AI tools for language editing or data analysis must be disclosed in the manuscript. AI systems cannot be credited as authors, and authors remain responsible for the integrity of the work.
